## Requirements for an original production

1. **Board the action, not just narration chunks.** For each beat choose a subject, intention, visible before → anticipation → interaction/contact → changed result → reaction/settle, and a meaningful hold. Draw establishing, action, detail and reaction shots where the story needs them. Plan the changing pose, direction, prop state, world and camera—not just a row of backgrounds. Background motion and character idle support, but do not replace, the main action. Intentional quiet holds are valid.
2. **Art-direct actual sets and assets.** Establish the camera, perspective or deliberate 2.5D layout, floor plane, foreground/midground/background, scale, light source, cast/contact shadow, practicals, surface detail and actor silhouette. Use a cohesive niche-appropriate palette and enough relevant set dressing for the location. Avoid a single flat rectangle with a generic gradient, a few unrelated icons or large empty stretches. Test at delivery size and phone size. Rebuild weak assets rather than adding particles or a global vignette.
3. **Build connected performance.** Keep the user-requested long-bodied human structure, with one arm behind and the other in front, unless the brief calls for another design. Faces need expressive eyes/brows or mouth visible at the chosen framing; action needs anticipation, intent, follow-through and weight. Subject motion, gaze, idle and reaction should relate to the story. Do not enforce a human character in character-free, animal or object-led projects.
4. **Make contacts real.** Author reusable pivots and hand/foot/prop anchors in the same world coordinates. Use an articulated chain with constrained lengths; if a target is unreachable, move the actor/target or revise staging—not stretch a limb or hide the error. Attach a carried prop to the appropriate hand and preserve world pose when reparenting. Lock a stance foot in world space until lift; lift and plant on actual surfaces. Respect depth order and masks at entrances, windows and handoffs. `../examples/contact-physics.js` is a limited two-bone helper, and `../examples/contact-light-study.html` is an original **silent technical study**, not an aesthetic benchmark or a finished video. The older rigs do not constrain limb lengths and must not be assumed production-ready.
5. **Use camera to support action.** Compose for readable action first. Establish spatial geography, then cut or move to detail or reaction at a motivated moment, including character-free inserts where appropriate. Keep enough overscan throughout the move. Avoid a single full-frame push applied to every shot; a magnified flat illustration is still flat. Layer and animate foreground separately when it improves depth.
6. **Create and reject tests honestly.** A representative test should use the actual project's art direction, a meaningful interaction and reaction, a second framing/shot, relevant background action, camera/light changes and requested audio. Test visibly distinct beginning, contact, result and transition. The included studies illustrate isolated mechanics and are **not** substitutes for this test. Do not scale up a test with detached props, floating/sliding feet, unnatural limb lengths, weak sets or mostly static poses. If unable to visually review actual motion and listen when sound is requested, mark those gates unknown; do not claim reference-level quality or pass them from a decode or plan alone.
7. **Inspect the real final.** Fully decode; examine the encoded output at representative timestamps and around all boundaries, with frame-by-frame review of hand/foot contacts and layer occlusion. Watch normal and slowed playback and listen to the actual mix if capable. Verify narration, captions and scene labels together. Record time-coded evidence, defects, repairs and unperformed checks. If the clip remains visibly glitchy or mostly static, fix and rerender; if that cannot be done, present it as an explicitly incomplete draft, not a finished reference-quality video.

## Numeric checks help, but are not visual approval

Export actual rendered rig points after all actor/parent transforms, before or with the inverse of the camera transform, to a single world-space coordinate system. Sample every output frame during critical holds and contacts. The optional MCP `check_motion_samples` compares caller-supplied two-segment lengths, contact gaps and fixed-target drift against explicit tolerances. It can flag chosen, sampled geometric errors; it cannot see pixels, detect missing/misnamed points, know whether an anchor or tolerance is correct, assess full coverage, or judge acting/lighting. A numeric pass is never a quality pass. The tool works without image generation, and the skill still works without MCP.
