# Scene and clothing selection

Choose scenes and clothing from the user’s prompt and the answers in `intake.md`; New project is the sampled quality target, not a reusable default setting. Apply the observed layered, actively animated cinematic treatment to new scenes by following `reference-quality.md` and `animation-blueprint.md`. Make these choices **before** drawing assets.

## Scene selection: from meaning to shots

1. Break the script into beats. For each beat write the action or concept, what changes, emotion, must-show object, actual time/place if factual, and narration cue. Do not pick a setting just because it looks cinematic.
2. Generate 2–3 possible ways to show each beat: a character/prop action, an establishing environment, an insert, a reaction, or a diagram if helpful. Choose the one that makes the idea clearest and fits the user's preferred backgrounds. If historical, match setting and era to sourced claims; label composite fiction. If no accurate location is known, show a generalized and explicitly illustrative environment.
3. Score each candidate qualitatively on **story relevance, viewer clarity, continuity, niche/audience, feasible motion, factual accuracy, visual variety, and user constraints**. Reject unrelated spectacle, confusing geography and repetitive tableaux. Do not present the score as objective proof.
4. Lay out a small reusable set library with consistent lighting and anchors. Order scenes by the story's causality, not by a checklist of attractive locations. Select shot size (wide / medium / detail), camera aim, action, framing/negative space, foreground and return point. A short can have fewer locations than a long explainer; do not enforce a fixed number of cuts.
5. On each hold, plan intentional idle and environmental life, plus a semantic event when needed: a glance, task, held prop, changing diagram, smoke/water or distant vehicle. A deliberate still moment is allowed. The character should interact when it helps tell the story; an animated background alone cannot replace the story beat.
6. Assign light per location: warm practical pool with a source, cool ambient sky, a contained motivated spotlight, rim and ground shadow, or a daylight shaft. Support the main action rather than painting a bright disc on a face. Use gentle camera push, pull or pan to direct attention; do not crop heads/feet, drift endlessly or shake without cause. Check real output and mobile readability.

| Prompt/beat | Candidate scene that carries it | Wardrobe/prop direction |
|---|---|---|
| Fictional wartime departure | Warm home doorway, then a period-appropriate station; a held bag makes departure visible | Simple period-inspired uniform and helmet for recruit; civilian clothing for family; do not imply a specific real unit. |
| Bakery order confusion | Shared counter with two overlapping lines; cut to order ticket, then return to the counter as the process changes | Apron for worker, distinct customer silhouettes, an order ticket. Fictional example is not a measured business result. |
| Forest animal rescue | Clearing establishes where the animal wants to go; branch/rope insert; return to coordinated action | Species-readable fur/feathers and body anatomy, not human clothing unless requested; preserve species cues. |
| Software tutorial | Device/workspace and clear illustrative interface; closer view of actual relevant control | Contemporary clothing, computer and legible UI; verify actual steps/version and avoid counterfeit branding. |

## Wardrobe selection: identity, context and rig

For each recurring character create a small clothing record: role, age presentation, setting/period/culture, relevant weather or activity, silhouette, palette, layers, shoes, accessories, source if factual, and continuity through scenes. Ask about clothing preferences when they materially affect the brief; otherwise make a suitable proposal. Never stereotype a culture or assign identity from clothing alone. Respect user-specified anatomy: for the long-body character, clothing is an editable surface over the **same rounded capsule**; keep back arm behind torso and front arm/hand in front.

- Make characters distinct with restrained color, collar, pockets, cuff or accessory rather than changing their proportions unexpectedly. Keep a controlled palette and adequate separation from the backdrop.
- Adapt clothing to role and action: clothing should move with its parent body part; a bag/letter belongs to the hand, not the screen. Avoid details so small they become visual noise.
- Historical uniforms, medical gear and protective equipment can imply accuracy: research required details or clearly label stylization and do not invent insignia or claims. Fictional costumes may be freely original within the brief.
- Before/after each wardrobe change, check that it is motivated by time/place, readable at output scale, keeps silhouette and limb attachment intact, and does not change unexpectedly during a cut.

## Production matrix

For each shot store `beat`, `location`, `why_this_scene`, `factual_or_fiction`, `shot_size`, `character`, `wardrobe`, `prop`, `before`, `action`, `after`, `light_source`, `camera_move`, `audio_cue`, and `continuity_checks`. Review the actual frames and movement; text alone does not prove the chosen scene was rendered or that a costume is historically correct. See `../examples/scene-wardrobe-template.md`.
