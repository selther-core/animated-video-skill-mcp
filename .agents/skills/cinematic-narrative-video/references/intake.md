# Prompt-first intake: installation, questions, production

## Installation is not production

When asked only to install the skill, install/configure it where permitted, verify discovery as possible, and stop. Do not ask for a topic or create a video during installation. Afterward, wait for the user to enter their own video prompt. If they supplied a production request already, do not make them repeat it.

## Read the prompt before asking

Extract the story/topic, intended audience, goals and constraints. Identify information already stated versus unknown. Then ask **one concise grouped round** covering these five decisions, suggesting context-aware defaults and allowing “surprise me” or “use your judgment”:

1. **Backgrounds:** Which places, visual world, mood or locations should appear? Any visual elements to include or avoid?
2. **Length:** About how long should the finished video be, and is it short-form or long-form?
3. **Video type:** Story, explainer, tutorial, documentary-style, commentary, advertisement or another form? What tone and format?
4. **Niche:** Which niche/topic area and audience should guide examples, facts, scenery and wardrobe?
5. **Voice:** Narrator or characters; preferred gender presentation, energy, mood and delivery? All spoken voices must use an American accent; English defaults to en-US. If a specific voice was already chosen, retain it unless asked to change.

Use the host's question UI when available; if its question limit is lower, split this single intake into the fewest grouped prompts or ask in text. Acknowledge answers already supplied (e.g. “You asked for 60 seconds—keep that?”) instead of demanding them again. If all five are already explicit, present a short confirmation with an easy correction/“proceed” option; do not block on redundant questions. If the user has explicitly said not to ask, use their details and state reasonable assumptions. A reply such as “choose for me” is an answer: choose appropriate options and proceed without another unnecessary approval. Only ask a follow-up when a missing answer materially prevents safe or useful production.

After answers, summarize the resolved brief in the project; **begin production** without requesting yet another general approval. Plan with `scene-wardrobe.md`, research when needed, draw assets, use native TTS/rendering where available, and follow actual user constraints. Ask permission only for operations requiring it, e.g. installing tools or publishing. The optional MCP creates a draft project only; it does not ask interactive questions or render video on its own. The calling agent must conduct the intake.

## Worked intake

User: “Make a short story about a fox helping a bird.”

Agent: “Before I make it: (1) forest clearing, treetops, or another background? (2) Rough length—30 seconds, a minute, or your choice? (3) Is this a gentle story, comedy, or something else? (4) Is the niche children’s fiction, nature education, or another audience? (5) What American-accented voice should tell it—warm and playful, calm, or another style? You can say ‘choose for me’ for any of these.”

After the answers, create the treatment and proceed. Do not inject the WWII example's battlefield, uniform or airstrike into an unrelated fox story.
