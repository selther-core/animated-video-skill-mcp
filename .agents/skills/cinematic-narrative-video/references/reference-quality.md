## Recreate the production grammar, not the subject

1. **Art-direct the room/world first.** Give each shot a background, midground and foreground; material or architectural detail in service of the location; a defined ground plane and a restrained cohesive palette. Use cool ambient fill plus motivated warm windows, lamps, fire or daylight appropriate to the brief. Add local light pools, soft falloff, subtle rim/contact shadows and atmosphere; protect face/text contrast. A vignette, one spotlight or flat background alone is insufficient. Never make the reference's café, classroom, colors, props or subject mandatory for unrelated niches.
2. **Build progression within a coherent shot.** For each beat, record subject, objective, before → action/contact → changed result → hold, and an actual narration cue. Stage meaningful within-shot state changes: an articulated actor and prop interaction, evolving diagram, vehicle/object moving with purpose, character reaction or other topic-specific action. Use secondary idle and environmental movement sparingly. A slide, caption change, particle loop, blink, atmospheric movement or camera push does not by itself count as action.
3. **Use motivated camera design.** Start with the full scene and safe action area. Hold while information is introduced; move or cut to an insert, reaction or change when the meaning warrants it; restore context where needed. Ease pan/zoom (e.g., smoothstep) and provide overscan. A 2–8% slow push is a suggested starting point, not a measured rule or substitute for action. Inspect whole zoom paths for blank edges, cropped labels and visibility.
4. **Act and animate with intent.** If people suit the project, use an original long capsule torso, round dark head, readable eyes, narrow legs, one arm behind and one in front, with clothing appropriate to the scene. Rigs must have meaningful separate pivots, expressive eyes/brows/mouth and prop attachment. Keep limbs natural and restrained; check planted feet and hand contact. Adapt anatomy for animals and other subjects; human figures are not mandatory.
5. **Time and verify speech, text and scenes together.** Measure actual narration/audio duration first. Give each semantic event a stable ID and assign narration cue(s), scene start/end, active title/date, visual state and caption cue(s) to it; derive timing from the same absolute clock. Do not allocate equal-duration scenes or use estimated word counts instead of real speech. Check every cue against its scene's topic/date, not just against total duration. Scene-boundary tests: just before, at and after each change, plus a middle timestamp for every scene. If a caption intentionally contrasts events or bridges shots, annotate why; do not fabricate synchronization from timestamps alone. The MCP `check_alignment` tool can flag *planned* event-ID/time conflicts; its inputs are supplied by the agent and it cannot see or hear the result.
6. **Create a real representative test.** Before full production make a short sequence in the new topic that shows an articulated action, developing scene/diagram, motivated camera change and proper light; add real voice/sound when requested. Use the original `../examples/progressive-quality-demo.html` as a concrete, editable, silent technique study, not a finished video or reference reconstruction. Evaluate actual output against the reference's density, lighting hierarchy, action, framing and continuity; if it looks like the old documentary, rebuild the scene instead of applying a global filter. Keep quiet holds and sensitive subject matter dignified.
7. **Review and repair the final encoded video.** Compare sampled opening, middle, ending and every scene transition against storyboard and cue map, then play and listen if capabilities allow. Report timestamps for lighting/action/camera and caption/label checks, evidence and failures; check safe frame bounds, anatomy, source claims, rights, captions and full decode. A failed semantic alignment or mostly static action sequence is a **fail**, not an acceptable finished video. Fix and rerender, or report the blocker and deliver a clearly marked partial. Full-speed motion, subjective visual quality, voice/audio and sync must be marked unknown if not actually checked.

## Optional MCP plan check

Supply `check_alignment` an object `plan` like `../examples/alignment-plan.json`: each `visuals` and `cues` entry has `event_id`, `start` and `end` on the same absolute timeline, plus a top-level `duration_seconds`. A normal cue must overlap matching visual event IDs for at least 90% of its duration. An intentional bridge may set `intentional_bridge: true` with a nonempty `bridge_reason`; it is then flagged for manual review, not proven correct. A missing or empty list is invalid. The checker detects conflicts **only in the caller's own timing data**—not whether labels are truthful or the encoded video actually follows the plan. A silent video should instead receive an applicable visual-timing review.
