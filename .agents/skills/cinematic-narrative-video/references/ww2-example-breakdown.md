# WWII cut: example structure and limitations

This secondary implementation example is the project-authored **WWII cut**; the current quality target is New project (`reference-quality.md`). The cut is 64.59 seconds, 1280×720, 30 fps. It is a fictional, stylized composite; the scenes do not identify a particular battle, soldier, ship, aircraft model or military unit. Ten example stills are included in `../reference-frames/`. The silent HTML visual source is included as `../examples/ww2-seekable-scene.html`; no encoded film or audio recording is bundled. Use `?t=44` or `window.seek(44)` for a precise state. Encoded frames were sampled and the full file decoded, but audio was not auditioned and subjective full-speed review remains unverified.

| Approx. time | Story/visual role | Useful reusable mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:06.2 | Aircraft, release, tracked fall, impact, title | A motivated visual hook with cause and consequence; track a prop without blank borders or abrupt release jumps. |
| 0:06.2–0:11.6 | Home departure | Establish a person and relationship at a small scale; warm practical light; use a gentle change of framing. |
| 0:11.6–0:18.9 | Station | A second location and train motion signal a journey; keep direction and character identity coherent. |
| 0:18.9–0:30.3 | Training, equipment, teamwork | Vary outdoor character action, equipment inserts and an informative stage; keep selected objects relevant to the narration. |
| 0:30.3–0:41.1 | Ship, route, deck, letter insert | Vehicle/environment and map cuts, followed by a human-scale prop and a return to the established deck. |
| 0:41.1–0:52.7 | Rainy field, aircraft, reaction | Distinct environment, depth/weather and character reaction; avoid graphic effects and label illustrative setting. |
| 0:52.7–1:04.59 | Service and remembrance | Transition from broad context into a quiet personal closing. |

## What to carry forward—and improve

Transfer the *method*, not the literal war imagery: a topic-relevant hook, a clear journey or argument, scene-specific wardrobe and backgrounds, readable small figures, insert/return continuity, animated environmental layers, motivated lighting, cautious motion, and a grounded ending. For unrelated niches choose different scenes and characters. The example still has limitations: procedural walks and idle gestures require perceptual review, some stages are relatively static, and the craft/audio have not received a complete human review. It is a usable example, not a quality guarantee or exact historical simulation.

For each project choose scenes by meaning and wardrobe by character/context as described in `scene-wardrobe.md`; map narrative states and seekable events per `story-structure.md` and `code-animation.md`. Listen to the actual finished mix when tools permit and record any unverified checks.
