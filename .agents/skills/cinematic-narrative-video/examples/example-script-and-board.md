# Example script, storyboard and TTS text

**Purpose:** original 39–48 second demonstration of the workflow, not a finished video or text from the source. Topic: “What if the Moon disappeared?” Audience: curious general viewers. Orientation: 16:9; 30 fps. Treat the timing as a starting edit, then retime to measured TTS audio. Fact-check every claim and its context before publishing; this is not a complete science treatment. For example, see NASA's [Moon and tides overview](https://science.nasa.gov/moon/tides/), and validate that the source remains available and supports the final phrasing.

All spoken roles use an American accent; select U.S. English (`en-US`) voices. Make every voice energetic, expressive, clearly articulated and prominent in the mix without shouting or clipping. For specific voice types and delivery by niche, see `../references/voice-direction.md`. These are nominal timings. At a natural speaking rate they may be shorter or longer. Generate individual TTS files, measure each, then adjust each scene end and the total duration; do not time-stretch speech to force these numbers. The line labeled V03 should use a second voice if available; if not, choose one narrator and restate it as a narrator's rhetorical question rather than pretending there are two voices.

## TTS-ready plain text

**V01 narrator:**

Imagine looking up tonight and finding the Moon gone. The sky would be darker, but the real surprise would be down here.

**V02 narrator:**

The Moon's gravity helps shape Earth's tides. But it isn't the only influence. The Sun pulls on our oceans, too.

**V03 character:**

Wait. So the tides wouldn't just stop?

**V04 narrator:**

Right. Solar tides would still exist. Their patterns and strength would differ from the combined tides we know today, and local geography would still matter.

**V05 narrator:**

That's only one piece of the puzzle. How would a moonless world change the nights, the oceans, and the life that depends on them?

## Edit and delivery notes

- Make entirely original characters and environment; maintain one character's silhouette across shots. Refer to `../assets/classroom-example.svg` and `../references/character-rig.md`.
- Render labels large and keep them on screen long enough to read. Do not present the sample art's layout or numerical story timestamps as discovered reference measurements.
- Caption spoken lines and any important non-speech sound. Check the factual wording and limitations with current sources; add relevant citations to the published description. The example is a hook and mini-explanation, not a complete answer to the broader hypothetical.
- Export the video's actual measured length, generate an original thumbnail such as the compositional study `../assets/thumbnail-example.svg`, provide a title, description, sources and conditional playlist suggestion. Only call a video final after checking visuals, audio, accessibility and licensing.
