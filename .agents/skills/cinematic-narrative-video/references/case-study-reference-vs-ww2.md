# Video structure audit: reference, skill and WWII cut

## Scope and confidence

The benchmark is the user-supplied **3:57.9 excerpt** of the 6:58 source video, not the unseen ending. For this audit I resampled the excerpt every five seconds, plus targeted one- or two-second sequences at 0–11, 47–69, 118–140 and 195–217 seconds; prior notes also record one-frame-per-second sampling and four selected one-second sequences at 30 fps. I sampled the 60.53-second WWII battlefield cut every two seconds and checked its editable timeline/source. This is an evidence-based visual sample—not full-speed viewing, every frame, or a listening review. Source and final audio were not directly auditioned, so delivery, music, mix and exact speech-to-picture sync remain **unverified**. Times are approximate; sampled timestamps are not necessarily exact cut boundaries.

Across these samples: restrained, consistent figures; multiple reusable locations; warm practical light against cool environments; clear board/presenter space; visual progression inside holds; explanatory inserts, examples, comparisons and qualifying labels. These are visual observations, not proof of the source's software, narration pacing, or soundtrack. The reference is an explanatory argument; its logic should be adapted—not imposed mechanically—on a story in another genre.

## Comparison and revised criteria

| Criterion | Existing skill before this audit | WWII cut, sampled result | Revised criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening and promise | Calls for a meaningful hook and premise. | **Present:** title and aircraft/impact, then a journey begins. It establishes subject, but is a different action-first hook from the reference. | Hook with a concrete situation or question tied to the video's actual promise; create curiosity without misleading or sensationalizing. |
| Argument and progression | Calls for beats and diagrams, but does not require an explicit idea-to-visual state map in each major sequence. | **Partial:** linear departure → training → crossing → field → remembrance. This is a coherent fictional itinerary, but the principal scenes usually introduce a location rather than develop a causal idea through successive visual states. | For an explainer, map question → initial model → complication → worked example/comparison → supported takeaway. For a narrative, use goal → obstacle → choice/action → consequence → resolution. Do not force one form onto every niche. |
| Held sets with progressive changes | Mentions sequential diagrams and holds, but the former primary working example gives little sustained multi-step demonstration. | **Partial:** equipment appears sequentially; route and service icons change. The training board is largely a list, while several scenes mostly hold a pose or slowly move scenery. | Design at least one **meaningful within-shot progression where the content calls for it**, with named pre-state, action, post-state and readable hold. Change reveals, props, interactions or consequences—not arbitrary movement on a timer. |
| Character/action/prop causality | Rig and attachment instructions are present. | **Partial:** the same long-body figures recur; a letter is held, followed by a symbolic oversized insert. Figures are often stationary and interactions are limited in the sampled frames. Motion quality at full speed is unverified. | Plan and inspect at least one purposeful gesture or interaction for each relevant dramatic beat. Attach a prop to the hand, account for placement/contact, and preserve the requested back-arm/body/front-arm depth. Use restraint, not static repetition. |
| Places, framing and continuity | Calls for layered locations, cutaways and lighting. | **Present but limited:** home, station, camp, ship, field, plane and closing scene; the letter insert returns to the ship. Scenes have varied environments but the overall staging is comparatively flat and often front-on. | Reuse established sets where helpful; establish a space, change meaningful focal scale and return without spatial confusion. Reserve detail and light around the story action rather than merely decorating all frames. |
| Evidence and readability | Sources and caption delivery are required. | **Partial:** externally supplied SRT and credits; some illustrated labels/summary cards. The sampled documentary-style graphics do not include embedded source notes or detailed qualifying annotations like some reference diagrams; the composite is labeled fictional in credits. Mobile readability was not independently tested. | For factual claims attach a source/qualification when useful and include full sources in delivery; flag hypothetical examples. Ensure actual captions are available and timed. Do not require an on-screen citation for every fictional shot. |
| Audio, feel and synchronization | Direct listening and sync review are required. | **Unverified:** visual samples and full decode do not establish audible quality or sync. | Listen to the source and output when authorized and possible; otherwise mark unknown. Never claim an emotional impact, exact voice or synchronization on metadata alone. |
| Quality gate | Calls for a voiced test and review. | **Not established:** samples and full decode were checked, but full-speed review, audio audition and user approval are not documented. | Use evidence-tagged pass/partial/fail/unknown by criterion, test actual encoded motion/audio, fix the highest-impact mismatch, and retain unresolved issues. A visually sampled partial cannot certify a finished match. |

**Conclusion:** The WWII cut follows several existing construction criteria, including a recurring stylized cast, location variety, a map/prop cutaway and layered atmosphere. It does **not demonstrate the reference's sustained, idea-driven visual development to the same extent** in the sampled sections, and its real-time motion/audio quality has not been verified. The correct change is to strengthen the skill's structure, shot-state planning, interaction and review gates—not to claim the cut already matches, copy the reference's science content, or assume a WW2 story must use a classroom.

## Changes made to the skill

The revised package adds a reference-structure guide, a reusable evidence-tagged evaluation rubric and beat/visual-event template, and a working silent example of an original progressive scene. The core skill and MCP guide access are updated to use those materials. The examples are production scaffolding, not evidence that new work automatically meets the standard. No source video or reference audio is included in the package.
