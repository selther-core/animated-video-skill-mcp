# Production blueprint: animated film, not a slideshow

## The actual target

The user's requested quality target is **New project**, not the earlier WWII film. Read `reference-quality.md` for the observed lighting, framing, within-shot action, documented documentary mismatches and evidence limits. The source clip, frames and audio are not bundled; never copy the user's reference media, characters, exact layouts or words without rights. Reproduce the **level and grammar**, not the literal café, classroom, subjects or palette, and tailor each new film to the actual niche. Quality cannot be guaranteed by instructions or a renderer.

For an original, editable *technique study*, inspect `../examples/progressive-quality-demo.html` at `?t=0`, `?t=5`, `?t=8`, `?t=12` or call `window.seek(seconds)`: it layers a lit world, two jointed figures, a developing diagram, an animated prop, a gesture and a motivated camera push. It is silent, only 16 seconds, has not been subjectively reviewed at full speed, and is not a finished video or an equivalent of the reference. The project-authored `../examples/ww2-seekable-scene.html` is a secondary example of timeline and cut construction, not the benchmark.

## Non-negotiable difference from a slideshow

A slideshow switches between static illustrations or text cards, often with a zoom, fade, labels, or narration. **That is not this skill's target.** A camera move, blinking, particle overlay, weather loop, captions and crossfade alone do not turn a static illustration into acting or storytelling. A valid animated scene has distinct separately controlled visual layers and a meaningful subject or prop changing position, pose, shape, relationship, contact, expression or state *within the scene*. Deliberate still holds and a specific quiet insert are allowed; they cannot be the whole video or replace the primary action. A media-inspection algorithm, a JSON plan and a successful encode cannot prove animation quality.

## Build the same production architecture

1. **Prompt → brief:** install only during setup; later collect backgrounds, duration, video type, niche/audience and voice in one grouped intake, respecting choices already supplied. Summarize and begin. Understand the subject before assigning locations and clothing.
2. **Beat → action → shot:** for each beat define what changes, who/what initiates it, start and end positions, interaction/contact, change in expression or scene state, hold, meaningful camera choice and actual measured audio cue, active scene label/date and caption on one absolute timeline. Use stable event IDs and audit each transition to avoid showing the wrong historical period or subject. Pick the setting, costume and props for this niche (`scene-wardrobe.md`); ensure a beginning, progression and payoff. For a 60-second film, plan sufficient consecutive action beats and intentional cutaways; do not fill the minute with a series of unrelated cards or stationary wide shots.
3. **Layered authored scenes:** build original editable SVG/HTML/CSS/Canvas. Each shot has a topic-specific background (sky/architecture/landscape), separate midground subject and foreground, recognizable props, scene-specific motivated light source, atmospheric depth, shadows and readable negative space. Use silhouettes and cinematic gradients comparable to the project source, changing color and location as story requires. Build complete environments; avoid blank backgrounds with floating icon/text.
4. **Actors and objects:** use editable jointed figures. When a human fits the brief, begin with the actual long rounded capsule torso, round dark head, expressive light eyes, slim legs, simple hands/feet and restrained action in `character-rig.md` and `../examples/staging-rig-demo.html`. Maintain **far arm behind torso and near arm in front**, attach props to a hand and keep feet grounded. Clothing, headwear, expression and context must match this new subject—not a default uniform. For animals/vehicles/nonhuman objects, construct topic-specific articulated or multi-part assets and animate their real action rather than forcing the human rig.
5. **Choreograph actual animation:** animate anticipation → movement → contact/action → result → settle. Meaningful examples: a figure walks to and picks up an object; a hand opens a letter and another reacts; a boat crosses a layered ocean; an aircraft releases and the camera follows an object; a mechanism actuates and changes a visible state. Adapt these *mechanisms*, never their literal WWII content. Use smooth time-based interpolation, small natural idle/blinks, pose-specific expressions and environment motion. Walking should match foot contact and travel; test it frame by frame for slipping. Strong moments need subject movement or visible changes, not just ambient movement.
6. **Cutting, camera and light:** select wide → subject action → insert/reaction → return when meaningful. Have established scene geography and controlled lighting per scene. Use modest motivated pans, pushes and object-follow moves with no exposed stage edge, cropping or unexplained shakes. The story can include breathing room, but the sequence should progress visibly. If wrapping an existing `window.seek`, save the original function first; seek the new opening, boundary and later scenes out of order to catch recursion and accidental repeats.
7. **Voice, sound, render:** when requested, synthesize the selected American-accent voice with native TTS, measure its timing, sync action to speech and mix suitably licensed audio. Render the actual seekable animation frame-by-frame, not a slideshow program. `../examples/render_seekable.py` is an optional generic renderer for a self-contained file that exports `window.seek(t)` and `window.DURATION`; it can mux an existing audio mix. It does not generate scenes, script, voices or audio. See its `--help` and `production-manual.md` for dependencies and limitations.

## Run the actual reference or your adapted scene

With Python Playwright, its Chromium browser and FFmpeg (`libx264`) available, the following command from the repository root renders **only a silent three-second original technique study**, not a final project:

```sh
python3 .agents/skills/cinematic-narrative-video/examples/render_seekable.py --html .agents/skills/cinematic-narrative-video/examples/progressive-quality-demo.html --output original-technique-study.mp4 --start 5 --duration 3
```

For a new film, author or adapt your own local HTML with `window.DURATION` and `window.seek(t)`, and pass that file to `--html`. Supply an already-timed, rights-cleared final audio mix with `--audio path/to/mix.wav` when requested; the renderer pads or trims it to the rendered frame duration, so verify its synchronization. It cannot invent the niche's scenes, subject motion or voice; those are the agent's job. It blocks network resource loads and refuses to overwrite an existing output. Use only trusted local HTML. If dependencies cannot be installed or run, provide the strongest honest partial and state the blocker.

## Anti-slideshow review and acceptance

- Before a full-length render, make a **representative animated test** using the project's own planned world: an action with a readable before/contact/result, animated subject and other scene layers, an expression/reaction or changed object state, a motivated camera move, and sound if requested. Short tests may be shorter than 15 seconds. If it is only static art with captions/zoom/weather, reject, fix and render again.
- Review the **actual encoded output** at beginning, mid-action, after action, later scenes, transitions and ending, including out-of-order deterministic seeks. At each transition inspect the actual visible scene, location/date label and caption against the spoken cue; a mismatch is a failure, even if file timing or the plan passes. In the quality report identify timestamps and describe which subject or object *actually changes* and why. Review playback in real time and listen to the final mix when tools allow; if unavailable mark those checks **unknown**, not pass. No pass based only on frame counts, file metadata, motion vectors or a storyboard.
- Check contact, anatomy, prop attachment, human/animal movement, expressions, text safe areas, transitions and scene backgrounds. Technical pass: stream metadata, complete duration, audio as requested and full decode. If the result is mostly title slides/static images or only camera motion over stills, **fail the style gate and revise**. If the problem cannot be fixed, deliver a clearly marked partial and say what is missing; don't call it a finished animation.
