# Reference-informed structure and production criteria

This is a flexible production grammar targeting the visual quality of New project, based on the sampled evidence and limits in `reference-quality.md`. The project-authored WWII cut is a secondary example, not the benchmark; neither reference audio nor full-speed subjective motion was directly reviewed in the comparison. Adapt structure and visuals to the user’s topic and medium; the war setting is not a default.

## A. Build the story before choosing shots

1. Write a one-sentence promise and what a viewer should understand or feel afterwards. Specify audience, genre, emotional limits, requested duration and source requirements.
2. Choose a structural spine suitable to the brief, rather than treating scenes as an itinerary:
   - **Explanatory:** question → initial intuition/model → complication → worked example or comparison → qualified takeaway.
   - **Narrative:** goal or need → obstacle → meaningful choice/action → consequence → resolution. A fictional historical composite must be identified as such.
   - **Tutorial:** desired result → setup and prerequisites → demonstrated steps → check/correction → next action. Health, financial and other high-stakes topics need appropriate expert review.
   These are adaptable patterns, not mandatory fixed acts. Compress for shorts, expand for longer work. Retain room for meaningful quiet and do not invent a conclusion about the reference's unseen ending.
3. For each major beat, specify the **viewer change** (new understanding, emotion or decision); the relevant line or dramatic cue; and the scene/shot purpose. Select appropriate evidence and distinguish fact, illustrative example and fiction.
4. Build a **visual state progression** where relevant: BEFORE (what exists), ACTION (who or what changes it), AFTER (the visible consequence), HOLD (how long the result remains readable). A held set may support several reasoning steps—e.g. a diagram grows, a prop moves, a comparison appears. A still reflective moment can be purposeful without fake movement.

## B. Map each shot to action and audio

For each shot record: `id`, start/end after conforming to actual audio, story function, location, framing and light, subject, before/action/after/hold, timed events, relevant speech/audio cue, source or fictional marker, and inspection evidence. Use `../examples/story-beat-template.json` as a draft and `../examples/progressive-scene-demo.html` for a runnable silent, code-drawn demonstration of states on one stage. A camera move, drifting fog or crossfade is an enhancement—not a substitute for the idea or action changing.

- **Hook and setup:** use a question, character situation, or concrete consequence; show what the video promises without dishonest shock.
- **Shared stage:** establish spatial context and consistent recurring characters; create room for the idea. Reuse a location and alter visual payloads rather than fabricating a new backdrop for every sentence.
- **Progressive explanation or action:** reveal the next causal piece or character action in the line's context; maintain object identity and previous states long enough to understand the new one. For examples, comparisons or data, validate accuracy and qualifiers.
- **Cutaways and returns:** use close prop inserts, an environment/vehicle, a visual metaphor or reaction for a reason. Preserve screen direction, prop continuity and return geography. Do not force a classroom or infographic into a narrative where it does not help.
- **Resolution:** provide the best-supported answer, consequence or emotional closure appropriate to the actual genre. Choose a conclusion appropriate to the user’s story and evidence.

## C. Character, environment and design requirements

Use `character-rig.md` for the user-specified round dark head, long rounded torso, thin legs and back arm → torso → front arm; preserve this geometry only when that design is requested. Assign a specific intention to each pose or gesture. Attach held props to the hand. For walking, tie planted foot contact to actual motion; review full speed and close-ups rather than relying solely on procedural math. Small natural movement is preferable to filler or constant large swinging.

Use `cinematic-staging.md` to build independent background, midground, action and foreground, with a source of light and a clear subject silhouette. Weather, vehicles and shadows should support the shot's story and geography. Provide readable labels/captions and cite or qualify factual content. Keep illustrative maps, flags, vehicles, scenes and numbers explicitly distinct from verified historical or technical claims. Make all art original; use licensed inputs only.

## D. Evidence-tagged review—not an automatic score

For each criterion mark **pass**, **partial**, **fail**, or **unknown**, with timestamp, the check actually performed, and the necessary fix. `unknown` is mandatory when evidence is absent; do not infer audio, full-speed motion, fidelity or approval from stills. An example:

| Criterion | Evidence needed | Potential failure / correction |
|---|---|---|
| Promise and structure | Script and sampled/played opening, mid-beat, conclusion | A topic label alone may not establish stakes; write a concrete question or goal. |
| In-shot progression | Before, middle and after frames plus motion playback when available | The set changes but the idea does not; write a state transition and readable hold. |
| Character action and contacts | Real-time/slow play and frames at reach, grip, transfer, release | Floating prop or feet; parent to hand, adjust paths and contacts. |
| Geography and light | Wide/insert/return and scene-specific illumination | Inconsistent direction or dark faces; preserve orientation and motivate light. |
| Factual support and readable text | Source ledger and delivery captions / actual viewing size | Unsupported or illegible labels; verify, qualify, resize and hold. |
| Sound and timing | Listening to actual final encode and speech-to-picture sync | If no hearing capability, status is unknown; do not certify levels by peaks alone. |
| Technical delivery | Full decode, streams, duration and captions | Repair missing streams or invalid timing; decoding does not prove creative quality. |

A single still, a timed storyboard or the optional MCP tool cannot prove an animated result follows the criteria. Produce a proportionate style test of the actual project, with voice only if requested; use the actual final encode for the final review. Note user feedback, changes, remaining defects and which parts were directly viewed or heard in `production-learning-notes.md` or the project's quality report.
